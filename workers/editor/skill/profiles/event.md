# Event profile

For footage of an event, activation or pop-up: loading in, setting up, the day itself, people. Use it when
`intake.md` says the project is an event recap. Pacing is the same as `build.md` (the table under "Pacing").

## What makes a moment worth using (strongest first)

1. **People in the moment**: a guest taking the first sip, a laugh, a hand reaching for the product.
2. **The peak**: the busiest, liveliest stretch; a crowd at the stand, a line forming.
3. **Product in action**: pouring, serving, handing over, a close-up of the thing being enjoyed.
4. **The finished setup**: the stand or space ready before doors open, a step back that shows scale.
5. **Getting there**: loading the truck, carrying pieces in, the night-before setup. A few beats, not the story.
6. **Details**: signage, branding, textures, used as one-shot breathers.

Counts against a moment: camera shake or refocusing, people walking through the frame, empty space where the
crowd should be, nothing happening, and the first and last second of a clip (phone being raised or lowered).

## Choosing the part of a clip

Never take a clip from its start by default. Sample it, find the frame where the moment happens (the sip, the
pour, the reaction), and start the shot just before it so the action lands inside the shot.

- **Real speed** for anything with people: reactions, serving, conversation, crowd energy. Speeding up people
  looks wrong. Keep these short (about 1s in a 15s cut) and cut on the action.
- **Sped up** only for: a pan across the setup (use the whole pan, 4x to 8x), loading or setup stretches
  (timelapse, 8x to 30x), and long locked-off crowd shots (8x to 16x).
- In a 15s cut, two or three sped-up shots at most. The rest are real-speed snippets.

## Structure

- **Hook**: the first one to two seconds is the peak moment or the most striking image (the finished stand with
  people at it), never loading in.
- **Order**: loose. Getting there (brief) -> finished setup -> the event building to its busiest -> a closing image
  (the setup at its best, or a last reaction). Best moments can move.
- **Variety**: alternate wide (the crowd, the setup) and close (hands, faces, product). No source range used twice.
- **Segments**: footage in groups (truck load, night before, event day) gets screen time by how good it is, not
  evenly. Event day usually earns most of it.
