# Hooks: opening words on screen

Every cut gets ~10 `hookOptions`: drafts of the opening on-screen text. The person picks one, writes their own, or
chooses none in the app; nothing is burned into the preview. Method adapted from `artemnovitckii/content-skills`
(viral-hooks, MIT); formulas in `hooks-formulas.md` are from `rediumvex/viral-hooks-skill` (MIT).

## What a hook has to do

In the first two seconds the viewer must know what the video is about and have one open question pulling them on.
Clear but flat fails. Clever but unclear fails.

## Four ways a hook fails (check every draft)

1. **Delay**: the topic arrives late. Cut everything before it. No greeting, no "so", no "wait till you see".
2. **Confusion**: it needs a second read. Short plain words, active voice, one idea.
3. **Irrelevance**: clear, but not about the viewer. Prefer "you/your" or a pain they already have. An "I" or "we"
   hook is fine for a story or confession when the viewer would want the same result or fear the same mistake.
4. **Disinterest**: nothing left open. Put what people assume (A) against what happened (B). The believable gap
   between them is the curiosity.

## Writing the ~10 `hookOptions`

1. Read `brand.voice`, `instructions` and `request` in `job.json`. Match the voice; reuse none of its wording verbatim.
2. Use only facts the brief gives or the footage plainly shows: real numbers, real days, real places, real client.
   Never invent a number, a result, a place, a name or a struggle. If the strongest hook needs a fact you do not
   have, write a different hook.
3. Draft from at least five different categories in `hooks-formulas.md`; do not hand over one idea reworded ten
   times. Treat the formulas as structures, never paste the wording. Fits for build and event videos: 004, 026
   (time-boxed), 043, 048 (cost and mistakes), 056 (guess the number), 062 (before and after), 073 (it's not A, it's
   B), 081, 083 (a specific number first), 100 (behind the scenes). Skip the LinkedIn-style and "industry doesn't
   want you to know" ones.
4. On-screen text is 3 to 9 words, readable in under two seconds, at most two lines.
5. It has to be true to what the video delivers. A hook the cut does not pay off loses the viewer at the reveal.
6. Revisions: keep the previous `hookOptions` unless the request is about the words.

## Not allowed

"Wait till you see this", "you won't believe", "nobody talks about this" with nothing named, "POV:" or "Story
time:" as empty wrappers, a call to action as the opener, shock the footage does not back up.
